The Life & Travels of Marco Polo

From 1254 to 1324, seventy years across the medieval world

Marco Polo was born in the merchant city of Venice in 1254 and grew up to make one of the longest journeys of the Middle Ages. At seventeen he left home with his father and uncle and traveled overland along the Silk Road, crossing Persia, the high mountains of Central Asia, and the deserts of western China to reach the court of the Mongol emperor Kublai Khan.

For about seventeen years Marco served the Khan, traveling on missions across the vast Mongol Empire and observing cities, customs, and trade that no European had described before. When at last the Polos were allowed to return, they sailed home by sea, threading through Southeast Asia and India before crossing Persia and the Black Sea back to Venice.

Marco's Journey

🎯 Mission Objective

Trace Marco Polo's remarkable twenty-four year journey across continents and seas, from his birthplace in Venice to the court of Kublai Khan and back again. As you explore each location, notice how the routes he chose, the challenges he faced, and the people and places he encountered shaped his observations and made him a bridge between two worlds.

🔍 Search Targets

Venice, Italy: Marco's home as a merchant family where his long journey began at age seventeen and where he returned after twenty-four years

Shangdu (Xanadu), Inner Mongolia: The Khan's summer palace and the destination of Marco's three-and-a-half-year overland crossing of the Silk Road

Hangzhou (Quinsai), Jiangsu: The former Song capital that Marco called the finest city in the world, with canals and bridges like his native Venice

Quanzhou (Zaiton), China: The mighty southern seaport where Marco sailed from China with the Khan's permission, starting his long voyage home

🧠 Critical Thinking

Traveler's Choice: When the Polos reached Hormuz on the Persian Gulf, they decided not to sail to China by sea but instead turned back to travel overland for three more years. What factors might have convinced them that the harder path was the better choice?

Record and Witness: Marco recorded details about paper money in Khanbaliq, pepper at Quanzhou, and spirit voices in the desert at Dunhuang. What made Marco such a careful observer, and how did his written descriptions change what Europeans believed about Asia?

Risk and Reward: The journey home by sea was supposed to be faster but brought sickness and storm deaths. Looking at the route from China through Sumatra, India, and Persia, what would you have chosen, and why?

📚 Key Vocabulary

Silk Road: A network of ancient overland trade routes connecting Europe with Asia, used by merchants to buy and sell goods like silk, spices, and gems for over a thousand years

Mongol Empire: A vast territory ruled by the Mongols from the 1200s to the 1300s that stretched from China to Persia and allowed merchants like Marco Polo safe passage across Asia

Caravan: A group of traders and merchants traveling together across deserts and mountains with goods and animals, moving in stages to reach distant markets

Oasis: A fertile area in a desert where water is found, allowing people to rest, grow food, and gather supplies before continuing across dry, empty lands
