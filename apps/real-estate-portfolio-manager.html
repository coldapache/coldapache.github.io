Investor's Quick Reference

Cap Rate (Capitalization Rate)
Measures how much return a property generates relative to its value. Higher = better return.
Formula: Annual NOI ÷ Property Value
NOI (Net Operating Income)
All rental income minus operating costs (taxes, insurance, maintenance). Does NOT include mortgage payments.
Formula: Annual Rent − Annual Operating Expenses
Cash Flow
The actual money left in your pocket each month after collecting rent and paying ALL bills, including the mortgage.
Formula: Rent − Mortgage − Expenses
Equity
The portion of the property you truly own. Grows as you pay down the mortgage and as the property appreciates.
Formula: Property Value − Mortgage Balance
LTV (Loan-to-Value)
Shows how leveraged you are. Higher LTV = more borrowed money = more risk (but also more potential upside).
Formula: Mortgage Balance ÷ Property Value
Cash-on-Cash Return
Measures the return on YOUR actual cash invested (the down payment), not the total property value.
Formula: Annual Cash Flow ÷ Total Cash Invested
Down Payment
Cash paid upfront when buying. For investment properties, lenders typically require 20% of the purchase price.
Mortgage
A loan used to buy property. You pay it back monthly over 30 years with interest. The payment includes principal (loan paydown) and interest (cost of borrowing).
Appreciation
When a property increases in value over time due to market conditions, neighborhood improvements, or renovations.
Vacancy Rate
The percentage of time a rental unit sits empty with no tenant paying rent. Lower is better for the investor.
Condition
The physical state of the property (0-100%). Low condition means higher expenses, harder to keep tenants, and potential for costly emergency repairs.
Refinancing
Replacing your current mortgage with a new one at a lower interest rate. Saves money monthly but has upfront closing costs (about 2%).

Teacher Resources

Learning Objectives

  • Understand core real estate investment terminology (cap rate, NOI, LTV, cash-on-cash return)
  • Calculate monthly cash flow, net operating income, and return on investment
  • Compare residential vs. commercial property investment characteristics
  • Evaluate risk factors: vacancy, maintenance, market fluctuations, leverage
  • Make data-driven property purchase and management decisions
  • Understand how mortgage financing and interest rates affect investment returns

Standards Alignment

  • CEE National Standards: Standard 10 (Institutions), Standard 11 (Money & Inflation), Standard 12 (Interest Rates)
  • Jump$tart Standards: Financial Responsibility, Saving & Investing, Credit & Debt
  • Common Core Math: 7.RP.A (Ratios & Proportional Relationships), HSA-CED (Creating Equations)
  • C3 Framework: D2.Eco.1 (Economic Decision Making), D2.Eco.6-8 (Markets)
  • NBEA: Personal Finance standards — Real Estate, Investment Analysis

How to Teach This

  • Hook Activity (10 min): Ask students: "If you had $100,000, how would you invest it?" List responses on the board. Introduce real estate as one investment vehicle and compare to stocks, bonds, savings accounts.
  • Guided Demo (15 min): Project the game on screen. Walk through purchasing one property together. Point out the P&L breakdown, explain each line item. Have students predict the monthly cash flow before revealing it.
  • Pair Play (20 min): Students play in pairs, taking turns making decisions. One student is the "analyst" (calculates ratios) while the other is the "decision maker" (chooses actions). Switch roles every 6 months in-game.
  • Think-Pair-Share: After playing 12 months, pairs discuss: "What was your best investment? Worst? Why?" Share with class. Connect to real-world examples.
  • Scaffolding Tips: For struggling students, start with only residential properties. For advanced students, challenge them to achieve positive cash flow on every property simultaneously.
  • Assessment Use: The built-in quiz tests vocabulary and calculation skills. Use the printable worksheet for formal assessment. Portfolio tab data can serve as evidence for performance-based assessment.

Discussion Questions

  1. Why might a property with a higher purchase price actually be a better investment than a cheaper one?
  2. How does leverage (using a mortgage vs. paying cash) affect both risk and potential return?
  3. What are the advantages and disadvantages of residential vs. commercial properties?
  4. How do interest rate changes affect real estate investors? What about renters?
  5. Why is diversification important in a real estate portfolio? What kinds of diversification exist?
  6. How is real estate investing different from buying stocks? What are the trade-offs?
  7. What ethical considerations should landlords think about when setting rent prices or screening tenants?
  8. How do local economic conditions (job growth, population changes) affect property values?

Key Vocabulary

Cap Rate: Net Operating Income divided by property value. Measures return independent of financing.

NOI (Net Operating Income): Total income minus operating expenses (excluding mortgage payments).

Cash-on-Cash Return: Annual pre-tax cash flow divided by total cash invested. Measures return on YOUR money.

LTV (Loan-to-Value): Mortgage balance divided by property value. Higher LTV = more leverage = more risk.

Cash Flow: Money remaining after all income is collected and all expenses (including mortgage) are paid.

Appreciation: Increase in property value over time due to market conditions or improvements.

Vacancy Rate: Percentage of time a rental unit is unoccupied. Lower is better.

Equity: Property value minus mortgage balance. The portion you actually own.

Refinancing: Replacing an existing mortgage with a new one, typically at a lower interest rate.

Down Payment: Cash paid upfront when purchasing. Typically 20-25% for investment properties.
